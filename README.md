# JSRF Mod Tool

Edits Jet Set Radio Future's game files for the [JSRF recomp](https://github.com/Miggas/JSRF-Recomp): textures (export, import, paint on them or on the 3D model), models (export to and import from Blender), materials and sounds. Windows and Linux (Steam Deck included).

An edited file is changed in place in the game folder. Before its first change, the original is kept in `mod_backups/` at the game's own path (`mod_backups/Media/Player/Beat.dat`), and **Restore original** puts it back (for a graffiti, its `.prs` too). The game keeps copies of most of its files in its cache (`savegame/Cache` in the game folder) and reads those after the first time: the tool changes that copy too when there is one. Nothing is needed from the recomp.

## Use

1. **Choose...** the game folder: the one with `default.xbe` and `Media`. Put the tool in the JSRF launcher's folder and it uses the launcher's game folder by itself (the one set in Play, else `jsrf/` beside the launcher). The tool looks through `Media` and lists every file with textures, in tabs: Characters (by their names in the game), Graffiti (in sections SS, S, M, L and XL: `grf_0_` to `grf_4_`, one entry a graffiti, by its number, its thumbnail at the right of its box; then the rivals' graffiti in Rokkaku Police (`rpolice_`) and Golden Rhinos (`sai_`), by size), Stages (one entry a stage: see below), Objects, Cutscenes, Menus & HUD (the graffiti editor's files among them), each in collapsible sections, closed at first. The graffiti sheets (`Mark/PRESS`) aren't listed.
2. Pick a file, then one of its textures.
3. **Export PNG** saves it to `texture_edits/` in the game folder (**Open edits folder** opens it).
4. Edit the PNG in any image editor and save it under the same name. Transparency is kept.
5. **Import PNG** puts it back. You can also drop an image on the window to import it into the picked texture: PNG, TGA, JPG, BMP, GIF (its first frame) or PSD (the merged image).

**Paint** opens the picked texture in the painter: Brush, Eraser (back to the original), Picker (a colour from the texture) and Fill, with size, hardness, opacity and fill tolerance, and the colour chooser. The mouse wheel zooms, a right drag moves the view, Undo / Redo (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) go back and forth. **Resize** makes the painting 2, 4 or 8 times bigger by integer scaling (each pixel a block, nothing blurred), so you can add finer detail; a size over 4096 (for graffiti, over 4 times the original) is greyed out. You paint on a full-quality copy: **Save** compresses it into the game file once, the same way an import does.

**3D view**, on the right (only for files with models: for the others, like graffiti and sounds, the texture or sound takes its room), shows the picked file's models that wear the picked texture, and follows the painting as you paint. Drag to turn the model, right drag to move it, the wheel zooms. **All** lists every model in the file, Reset puts the camera back. **Wires** shows the model's triangles, on the model and over the texture (in the painter too), so you can see which part of the texture lands where. Menus and HUD files don't say which model wears which texture, so there the view stays empty unless All is ticked.

**Paint** in the 3D view paints straight on the model, with the painter's tool, colour and size (ticking it opens the painter if it isn't open). The brush's circle shows its size on the model where the cursor is. A stroke that crosses onto another part of the texture carries on there, without a line through the texture between. The picker and fill work on the model too. Middle drag turns the model while Paint is on, right drag moves it, the wheel zooms. Undo, redo and Save are the painter's: it is one painting, whichever side you paint on.

**Export** in the 3D view saves the model shown in `model_exports/` in the game folder, in two forms: a Wavefront `.obj` (with its `.mtl` and its texture as a PNG: the painting, while painting it) and a Valve `.smd` with its skeleton and bone weights. Open the `.obj` in Blender with File > Import > Wavefront; the `.smd` needs Blender's Source Tools add-on and comes in rigged, with each vertex's weights.

**Import** puts a model back: it reads whichever of the exported `.obj` or `.smd` was saved last, or drop any `.obj` or `.smd` on the window for the model shown. The model's bones, shadow shapes and materials are kept; its visible mesh is replaced. Keep the material names (`mat0`, `mat1`...) to keep each part's material; other names take the original's materials in turn.
- From an `.smd`, each vertex gets the bones and weights in the file (the bones are the model's parts by number, as in JSRF ModTool's `.smd` files, so models made for it import as they are). Up to the game's limit for that model: Beat and the other characters take two bones a vertex, the heaviest two are kept.
- From an `.obj` (no weights in it), each vertex takes its bones from the nearest original vertex: fine for a character edited in place.
- At most 65535 vertices. For the `.obj`, keep Blender's export defaults (+Y up, -Z forward), with UVs and normals.

**Materials**, under the 3D view: the shown model's materials. Pick one (Highlight dims the rest of the model, to see which parts use it), change its colour (it multiplies the texture: white leaves it as it is), its shading kind (the kinds JSRF ModTool names: Standard, Skin, Lambert, Metal, reflective metal, semi-transparent, self-illuminated, UV-scrolling), and the three other numbers it holds (what they do isn't known). The 3D view previews the colours; **Save materials** writes them to the game file, **Revert** throws them away.

**Bounds**, under the 3D view: the sphere the game draws the model within (its "draw distance"). A model made bigger than its bounds can pop out of view early: **Bigger** doubles them, **Fit** makes them fit the model again (Import does that by itself).


**Stages** (m26; the stage editor's first step): the Stages tab lists one entry a stage (`StgNN` with its map's name; then those in `Stage/Custom`), and picking one opens the whole stage in 3D: its stage models and props with their textures, as the game places them, its **shadows**, cast on the surfaces as in the game (from the buildings' shadow volumes: the shapes stretched along the light, which **Shadow volumes** shows), its **grind rails** (yellow) and its **collision**, coloured by surface (floor, wall, stairs, half-pipe, ceiling, untouchable, ramp...; each one can be hidden). Over the models the collision is laid half see-through, alone it's solid. Point at anything to see what it is (a model's texture, a collision triangle's surface and flags). Left drag looks around; W A S D move (Shift faster), Q and E go down and up, the wheel goes forward and back, a right drag moves sideways and up; **Overview** and **From above** put the camera back. **Export as .obj** writes the stage to `stage_exports/StgNN/` in the game folder: `StgNN_visual.obj` (with its textures as PNGs), `StgNN_collision.obj` (a material a surface) and `StgNN_rails.obj` (a line a rail), in the game's own coordinates as the model exports are. The stage's files are listed under the view, to open one for its textures. Nothing is changed in the game files: the viewer only reads them.

**Edit** (m28; the stage editor's third step, props first): click a prop in the stage view to pick it (it turns orange, with a blue box round it). Then **Grab** (G) makes it follow the mouse at its height, a click drops it, right click or Esc puts it back; X, Y, Z and Facing can be typed or stepped; **Turn** (`[` and `]`, with Shift 90 degrees) turns it about the vertical; Page Up / Page Down raise and lower it (Shift: ten times as far); **Copy** (Ctrl+D) puts a copy beside it; **Delete** (Del) removes it; **Undo** (Ctrl+Z) goes back a step at a time. **Save the stage** writes the stage's `.bin` with the changes through the rebuild below, the original kept in `mod_backups` (Restore all or **Put the originals back** returns it). A copy stays in its original's area, the part of the stage it's drawn with. Leaving with unsaved changes asks first.

**Map pieces** (m29): the map's own geometry can be picked too. A click on a building, a roof, a pillar, a sign picks one piece (one connected part of the stage's models; see-through parts and, with Shadow volumes shown, shadows too); **Shift+click** adds or takes away pieces; **Add touching** (T) adds the parts touching what's picked (a roof's posts, say), **Add inside** (I) the parts inside its box. Picked pieces move, turn (about their middle), rise and fall, are copied (Ctrl+D; m29g) and deleted the same way as props. A copy of map pieces is saved as a new stage model (in the `.dat` holding the stage's last model, placed in its source's area), with copies of its collision and rails. The collision inside a piece moves with it (shown orange with Collision on), and so do the grind rails on it; a deleted piece's collision and rails go too. Saving writes the changed `.dat` files (vertex positions, normals, the spheres the game culls by) and the `.bin` (collision, rails), originals kept. Shadows (the shadow volumes) stay where they were unless picked and moved with the rest.

**Rebuild** (m27; the stage editor's second step), under the stage's controls: the tool reads every part of the stage's files (collision areas, models, vertices and triangles; the grind rails and the lists that find them; the areas with their placements, portals and visible lists; each `.dat` file's blocks) and writes the files again from those parts, working out every offset anew, as the editor will need to once things can be changed. **Check: rebuild, compare** writes nothing and says how many files came out identical to the game's. **Test: write it, parts moved** writes the stage's `.bin` with every part in another place (the shared vertices, triangles, rail points, portals and visible lists in reverse order, the blocks further apart): the game reads it only through the offsets, so the stage should play just as before; the original is kept in `mod_backups` first. **Put the originals back** returns them (Restore all does too).

**Restore all** (top right) puts back the original of every modded file in `mod_backups/`, and the game's cached copies, after asking once more.

**Sounds** (the Sounds tab, and any file with sounds in it): each sound's length and rate, its waveform, **Play**, **Export WAV** (into `sound_edits/` in the game folder), **Export all**, and **Import WAV** (the exported file, or drop any WAV on the window). An imported WAV can be any length, rate or channel count: it's made the game's rate and channels and its format (Xbox ADPCM or PCM), and the file around it is rebuilt to fit. The original is kept in `mod_backups/` as for textures.

**Music** (m14): the `.adx` files in `Media/Z_ADX` are in the Sounds tab too: **Music** (BGM), **Ambience** (Env) and **DJ (Professor K)**. They play, export and import like the other sounds. An imported song can be any length: it's encoded as ADX at the original's rate and channels. A song that loops in the game (the green lines on its waveform) loops the whole new song. Encrypted ADX files aren't read (JSRF's aren't).

Sizes: an image the texture's own size goes in as it is. A texture in a `.dat` can be made bigger: import a square image of 512, 1024, 2048 or 4096 pixels (larger than the texture) and the file makes room for it; the game reads the new size from the texture itself. Any other size is resized to the texture's (Lanczos). Graffiti (`.grf`, `.prs`, `.JTX`) can be made 2 or 4 times as wide and tall, up to 4 times their original size (a 128x128 graffiti up to 512x512, a 512x256 one up to 2048x1024), by import or by the painter's Resize. An image of any size can be imported into a graffiti: it is made the biggest of those sizes it fits in without being enlarged (for a 128x128 graffiti: 600x600 becomes 512x512, 400x400 becomes 256x256, 100x100 becomes 128x128), shrunk or enlarged with Lanczos, its shape kept and centred, transparent around it (600x300 becomes 512x256 in the middle of a 512x512 graffiti). Their files don't say their size (only the `.grf`'s TGA header does), so the original game can't read a bigger one. The JSRF recomp (v310k and later) shows a bigger graffiti from its `.grf` alone, in the stages (without compression) and in the graffiti select, so the list shows each graffiti's `.grf`, and a picture saved into it (import or painter) is saved into its `.prs` too, at the `.prs`'s own size: the game's own small copy, seen on the original game and wherever the recomp doesn't swap in the big one. Both originals are kept in `mod_backups/`; `.JTX` sheets can't be shown bigger. The tool reads a grown `.prs` by its `.grf`'s size times 2 or 4, and a grown `.JTX` as 2048 or 4096 square.

What it reads: characters, objects, stages, HUD sprites, fonts and logos (`.dat`), graffiti (`.grf`, `.prs`, `.JTX`), and music (`.adx`). Textures in DXT1, DXT3 and DXT5 are written back in the same format, with their smaller mip levels made again from the new image.

## Build

- Windows: `build_modtool.bat`
- Linux: `./build_modtool.sh` (needs SDL2, which SteamOS and desktop Linux have)

Both download Zig (the compiler) into `deps/` the first time, the same way the launcher does.
